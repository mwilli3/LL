# PRODUCT.md

## Product Purpose

LoveLarice Wellness is a nervous-system wellness brand for women who have outgrown reactive, hustle-coded wellness culture. The product is a triad of three pillars (Regulated Living, Rooted Wellness, Reclaimed Power), each anchored by an "archetype kit": a 30-day program with AI-guided pattern analysis, daily rituals, mantras, and an aligned supplement protocol. The archetype quiz is the front door: an eight-question nervous-system pattern assessment that sorts a visitor into one of three archetypes and matches her to the kit, content, and supplement that fit her dysregulation pattern.

The brand reads editorial, not clinical. The promise is regulation, not optimization. The voice does not shout.

## Register

**brand** — design IS the product. Marketing surfaces (the home page, the quiz, the campaign pages, the long-form content) carry the entire emotional case for the brand. Product surfaces (Shopify checkout, account, post-purchase dashboards) are downstream and should defer to the brand register established here.

## Users

A woman, late twenties to mid forties. She is high-functioning and has tried wellness culture without lasting results: programs she didn't finish, supplements that gathered dust, breathwork apps she stopped opening. She is articulate about her own patterns and skeptical of optimization language. She reads, journals, screenshots quotes. She is the person friends lean on.

She is not looking for a quick fix. She is looking for an honest mirror and a system that respects the complexity of her nervous system.

## Brand

**Voice:** editorial, warm, neuroscience-literate without being clinical. Confident, direct, never breathless. Uses italics for emphasis, never bold. Never uses em dashes (commas, colons, periods carry the weight). Never uses "just," "simply," or "easy."

**Tone shifts by surface:**
- Home page and quiz reveal: literary, declarative, almost liturgical.
- Pattern insight blocks: clinical-adjacent, naming the mechanism without jargon.
- Trust/checkout copy: practical, plain, never salesy.

**Lexicon (use):** regulation, rooted, reclaimed, sovereign, foundation, evidence, pattern, ritual, threshold, capacity, nervous system, downregulation, fawn response, window of tolerance.

**Lexicon (refuse):** girlboss, biohack, optimize, crush, slay, level up, unlock your potential, transformation journey, badass, abundance mindset (as filler), manifest (as filler).

## Strategic Principles

1. **The mirror beats the pitch.** The quiz result page is designed to feel seen first and sold to second. The offer earns its place by following the pattern insight, not preceding it.
2. **Regulation, not optimization.** Every word, color, and motion choice should signal calm capacity, not productivity gain. If a design choice would feel at home on a SaaS landing page, it does not belong here.
3. **Editorial restraint over wellness maximalism.** No leaf glyphs, no soft-focus stock women in white linen, no green-and-cream watercolor textures. The brand is closer to a literary journal than a wellness Instagram.
4. **Three archetype colors, used surgically.** Teal (Regulator), olive (Rooted), burgundy (Reclaimer) appear as accent rules and eyebrows only, never as drenched surfaces. The dominant surface is always cream, warm-beige, or dark-brown.
5. **Slowness is a feature.** Reveal animations use long durations (700ms+) and exponential ease-out curves. The breath pacer loop is eleven seconds. Nothing snaps.

## Anti-References

What this brand is **not**:
- Headspace / Calm (too clinical, too cool-blue, app-shaped).
- Goop (too aspirational-luxe, too white).
- Athletic Greens / Ritual / Care/of (too SaaS-supplement, too pastel-cream identikit).
- Glossier (too pink, too youth-coded).
- The Class by Taryn Toomey (too movement-coded, too NYC).
- The Nap Ministry aesthetic (right philosophy, wrong visual vocabulary).
- Any wellness brand whose home page leads with a hero metric, a circular gradient, or a soft-focus woman drinking tea.

What it borrows from (in spirit, not appearance):
- The typographic confidence of literary magazines (The Paris Review, Cabinet).
- The material restraint of Aesop's print collateral.
- The pacing of a long-form essay.

## Aesthetic Lanes That Would Be Wrong

Avoid these "first reflexes" for the wellness category:
- White + sage green + serif italic display (over-saturated lane).
- Cream + terracotta + handwritten script (Pinterest-coded).
- Dark forest green + brass + tarot imagery (too occult).
- Soft pink + lavender + bubble shapes (too app-coded).

The lane LoveLarice owns: cream + warm-brown + gold accent, with archetype hues (teal/olive/burgundy) as analytical accents only. Editorial spacing, Cormorant italic emphasis, no decorative imagery beyond minimal geometric archetype marks.
